# Architecture

> Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-04. Add new notes for this area here, not to `CLAUDE.md`.

**Workspace layout:** `pnpm-workspace.yaml` wires `apps/*` and `packages/*`. Packages export raw TypeScript directly (`"exports": "./src/index.ts"`, no build step) and are consumed by `apps/web` and `apps/worker` via `workspace:*`. `better-sqlite3` and `esbuild` need `onlyBuiltDependencies` approval in `pnpm-workspace.yaml`. `apps/assess` is the public quick test (P-L1 of docs/beta-launch.md; deploy in docs/assess-deploy.md): 36 situations, nine per dimension across writing, research, code, visuals, video, learning, planning, people, money and daily life, each an A/B choice answered on five circles (big at both ends, smaller towards the middle) (`QUIZ_ITEMS`, `quizResult`: the mean towards the right pole, an exact middle going to the side more answers leaned to; redesigned 2026-10-24 to ask 「你在 AI 时代会是什么样的超级个体」 — every earlier result was deleted by assess migration 7, as migration 6 did on 2026-10-17) → appearance → email gate (the result is made and the figure starts drawing) → what they want to do (chips plus a ≤60-character line; written combination, else an optional OpenAI-compatible text model off unless `ASSESS_TEXT_*` is set, else rules — `@crossline/core/assess-wants`) → a server-rendered 3:4 type poster (2026-10-24: the type's name and quote with no letters beside it, the four dimensions with the letters only as small coordinates, 「AI 替你干的」 from the person's answers (`aiDoes`, kept on the result) and 「你不让 AI 碰的」 (`AI_KEEPS`), the type's kindred person with one sourced line (`TYPE_KINDRED`, `assess-kindred.ts`), best/hardest partner, the invite QR bottom left and the WeChat group's QR bottom right — a transparent PNG at `apps/assess/assets/group-qr.png` or `ASSESS_GROUP_QR_FILE`, swapped without a code change when the group code expires), the three lines and their loop on the long image, plus a no-account pair card made when a friend answers through the forwarded invite link. It is its own small Next server (`output: "standalone"`, Docker, one Linux VPS behind Caddy) with its own SQLite file (`apps/assess/server/`: `store.ts`, `mail.ts` over SMTP, `security.ts` rate limits and honeypot, `purge.ts` 90-day retention), posters by satori + resvg with subset Noto fonts (`server/poster/`; `satori` must stay in `serverExternalPackages` — bundled, it cannot find its own `.wasm`), and figures drawn from the type's template by an image model only when `ASSESS_IMAGE_API_KEY` is set (`server/avatar/`; per address `AVATARS_PER_EMAIL`, counted under an HMAC of the address in `avatar_draws` so deleting does not give one back; templates in `apps/assess/templates/` are `<CODE>-female.png`/`<CODE>-male.png`, full length 2:3 in the MBTI-avatar low-poly style the owner chose, picked by `templateVariant` from the person's 女生/男生/中性 answer; the 32 were drawn through the owner's own Codex (`scripts/assess-templates.ts candidates --via=codex`, numbered `sheet`, `redo N`, `pick`) and chosen on 2026-10-15 — angular faceted low-poly, one Morandi tone per group from `TYPE_GROUPS`). Type content and relation rules are pure in `@crossline/core/assess-content`; the contract is `apps/assess/lib/contract.ts`; anything another person sees is keyed by the invite token, never the result token. It never touches the private app's ledger, models or accounts; keep it that way, since it is the one thing meant to be hosted publicly while the private app's `external` mode stays blocked.

**The day head clock (`apps/web/app/analog-clock.tsx`):** a real dial — ticks, numerals, hour/minute hands, a green second hand — placed from the same instant the rest of the page uses, read in `CROSSLINE_TIME_ZONE` (falling back to `America/Chicago`) rather than the browser's own zone so the dial and the 「周三 11:25」 label cannot disagree. Under `CROSSLINE_FIXED_NOW` it does not tick and drops the second hand, which is what keeps the visual-regression snapshot reproducible. It replaced a bare circle with one motionless dot that carried `aria-label="此刻 …"` and showed nothing. Since 2026-10-28 it sits in Today's head band (`app/today/day-head.tsx`) beside `DigitalClock` (the same instant in digits, `07:20:48`, held at the minute when pinned), the big date and the week strip.

**Data flow:** `apps/web/lib/crossline.ts` is the sole server-side entry point into persistence for the web app — it lazily creates a singleton `CrosslineStore` (SQLite, WAL mode) rooted at `CROSSLINE_DATA_DIR` (default `../../data` relative to `apps/web`), seeds starter milestones/relations/work-orders/a draft plan on first run, and exposes read/write helpers used by the Next.js route handlers under `apps/web/app/api/`. `CROSSLINE_FIXED_NOW` overrides the dashboard's notion of "now" (used by e2e tests for deterministic snapshots). **The person's zone is the device's** (2026-10-21): every page in `AppShell` posts `Intl`'s zone to `/api/settings/time-zone` once per tab session (`TimeZoneSync`, off under `CROSSLINE_FIXED_NOW`), and `syncDeviceTimeZone` writes it into `schedule.preferences.timeZone` whenever it differs, which every web and worker reader takes before `CROSSLINE_TIME_ZONE`. The hosted beta's env says `Asia/Shanghai`, and saving any schedule number had copied that default into the preferences, so a person in Chicago was planned and shown on Hong Kong's clock. The device's zone and not an IP lookup: a VPN or proxy moves an IP's place, and an IP lookup would need a third-party service or a GeoIP database; `CROSSLINE_PRIMARY_CLOCK_ID` (default `research`) picks which identity gets deadline-tier priority as the master clock.

**Event-sourced ledger (`packages/db`):** `CrosslineStore` owns the SQLite schema: an append-only `events` table keyed by `(workspace_id, idempotency_key)` plus current-state tables led by `work_orders`, versioned milestones, relations, plans, executions, evidence, capacity and connector bindings. `StoredTask` is gone. If an old `tasks` table exists, open-time migration first copies a file DB to `.v1.bak` or `.pre-r2.bak`, migrates non-health rows to WorkOrders and health rows to capacity commitments, then drops the legacy table. New databases never create it. `exportSnapshot()` returns typed current models plus the generic workspace-scoped table dump.

**Migrations are versioned and transactional.** `#runMigrations` walks an ordered array gated on `PRAGMA user_version`, runs each migration **whole, inside one transaction**, and writes the new version in that same transaction; before the first pending one it force-copies the file to `<path>.v<current>.bak`. This replaced a `PRAGMA table_info` guard that checked column A and then added A *and* B — a crash between the two `exec`s left the guard satisfied and the database permanently half-migrated with no way to notice. That matters more than it looks: shipping to anyone else means running schema changes on **their** database, which is the real productization blocker, not the engine. Migration 4 dropped six tables that were never written (`outbox`, `identities`, `projects`, `milestone_versions`, `insight_proposals`, `skill_versions`) plus `energy_observations.expires_at`, and added nine indexes. `importSnapshot` still accepts a snapshot taken before it: the retired names are skipped when empty and refused (`RESTORE_RETIRED_TABLE_NOT_EMPTY` / `RESTORE_RETIRED_COLUMN_NOT_EMPTY`) when not, because a backup that cannot be restored is not a backup. Deliberately **no** `busy_timeout` and **no** `journal_size_limit`: both were added, then measured out as non-problems (better-sqlite3 already defaults the busy timeout to 5000ms, and the oversized WAL beside a small database is the autocheckpoint threshold doing its job, not a missing checkpoint).

`listEvents(workspaceId, { type?, entityId? })` takes its scope and its filter in SQL. It used to take **no arguments at all** — every one of ~40 call sites read the whole ledger and `JSON.parse`d each row to filter in JS, three of them without filtering by workspace at all. Foreign keys are deliberately still absent (adding one to SQLite means rebuilding the table, and the `(id, version)` primary key on `milestones` blocks the most important one); `pnpm doctor:ledger` scans for orphan rows, half-migrations, retired tables and WAL size instead, and exits non-zero on findings. `applyMilestoneGate`, `revokeAutoAcceptance`, `refuteMilestoneRelation` and `resolvePlanChange` are each wrapped in one `.immediate()` transaction — all four were check-then-act sequences, and the 「账本写了已验收、作业还在 evidence_review」 split state was that, not a logic bug. `packages/db/src/isolation.test.ts` is what proves the `workspace_id` discipline actually holds.

**Offline command replay:** `CommandEnvelope` is validated and posted to `apps/web/app/api/commands/route.ts`, which re-checks scope and currently permits only `task.capture`. `clientCommandId` is the ledger idempotency key. Completion is intentionally not an offline shortcut: Today links to Workbench, where execution and evidence follow the WorkOrder state machine.

**Natural-language capture:** `addCapturedTaskWithId` in `apps/web/lib/crossline.ts` writes one `captures` row and nothing else — no identity, no estimate, no `Task` and no `WorkOrder`. Classification only happens when an explicit Analyze runs the `crossline-automation-stripper` Skill through the worker-owned Codex app-server (`apps/worker/src/analysis.ts`, `packages/codex/src/analysis.ts`) and the result passes `validateAnalysisProposal` (`packages/core/src/analysis.ts`); only then does `transitionWorkOrder` atomically promote the capture to an `awaiting_plan_approval` WorkOrder. A failed, refused, timed-out or unauthenticated analysis leaves a typed blocker on the capture, and a blocked capture can be re-analysed (each attempt gets its own idempotency key via `captures.attempt`). There is no keyword-regex identity guess and no 25/30/60 minute default anywhere in this path.

**Work orders:** WorkOrder is the only executable model: `captured → analyzing → awaiting_plan_approval → approved → preparing → ready_for_human → active → evidence_review → accepted`, plus `blocked`/`archived`. All execution and evidence changes route through `transitionWorkOrder`; a milestone-bound WorkOrder cannot be accepted with zero evidence. `archiveWorkOrder` (the 丢弃 button, reason required) is the only thing that emits `archived`; discarded orders leave the board for a drawer, never the ledger. Starting an order that already has materials on disk skips `preparing` — going back there would strand it, because the worker skips orders that already have materials.

**Pressing 完成 is acceptance; requests for a fact are steps; the board is merged daily (the owner's decisions of 2026-10-15).** `completeWorkOrderExecution` runs `human.completed` and then `evidence.accepted` with `onCompletion: true` — the one acceptance the `EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` guard lets through without evidence, milestone-bound or not; the gate now judges only what the person did not do themselves (the assistant's `agent.delivered`). It takes an optional `outcome {level: smooth|barely|gaps, note}` (the 「完成情况」; routes read it with `readCompletionOutcome`) and returns `completion {mode, followUp?}`. `completionRule` (`packages/core/src/completion.ts`) takes the person's choice (`payload.completionMode`, the 「完成方式」 switch on the proposal card and the task, `POST /api/work-orders/[id]/completion-mode`) over the judgement of the model that wrote the task (`payload.completion` `{mode, reason, why, question, askAfterHours}`, a required field of the analysis, backplan and step-plan schemas — the step planner's only fills a task that has none) over the old title rules (left for tasks written before it): handing in, homework, sending, attending, filing and practice are `self_evident`, and a result somebody else judges later (an exam's score, a competition round), hard-to-read quality and exploration `needs_assessment`: those write `assessment.pending`, which `apps/web/lib/outcome-check.ts` turns into an `outcome_check` ask — kept out of 待决, Today and every count (`listed` in lib/asks.ts), answerable from the idle-time `outcome_check` nudge (never urgent, so never busy or at night) or by typing —; 「结果还没出来」 with a date asks again that day, and a score against the milestone's one threshold writes `milestone_gate.judged` with `source: "person_outcome"`. The acceptance standard is frozen at `plan.approved` (`payload.acceptanceStandard`; `approveWorkOrderProposal(…, {acceptanceStandard})` takes the person's edited lines) and the gate is told to add nothing to it. A request for a fact (`packages/core/src/info-request.ts`: purpose is one fact or confirmation someone else holds, product a message plus reply, ≤15 minutes of the person's time, it unblocks other work) is never a task: the backplanner folds it into the task that needs it (`foldInfoRequests`, `payload.infoRequests`), the step planner plans it as an assistant-drafted email (never sent) plus the person's send, normalization keeps the send with the person, and analysis refuses an automation part that sends. Duplicates are first prevented where tasks are written (`apps/worker/src/duplicate-guard.ts`): the capture analysis and the backplanner are shown the open tasks (`existingTasks`) and name the one an item only adds to (`extendsTaskId`), and every new task — dates-pair homework too — is compared with the open ones by `duplicateOf` (`planMerges`' own rules) and folded into the one it duplicates or adds to (`task.duplicate_prevented`; an addition becomes a replan request on the kept task; an analysed capture is merged undoably, a backplan item is never created and its dependants depend on the kept task). `dedupePass` (`apps/worker/src/dedupe.ts`, once a day per workspace) folds standalone requests into the task they serve and merges duplicates by `planMerges` (same line and project, normalized-title Dice ≥ 0.6 with intents agreeing; different numbers, due days a week apart, or milestones whose deadlines are over 21 days apart never), asks `crossline-validation-reviewer` (`TASK_MERGE_REVIEW_SCHEMA`, discretionary spend) about pairs the rules leave open, keeps the task with the most progress, archives the rest `bySystem` with 「与「X」合并」 (shown in the 已归档 drawer as 「已合并」 with 撤销合并, `undoMerge`), records each merge as an undoable `task.unmerge` op and says it once with `sayToPerson`.

**Approval policy is centralized:** `requiredApproval(action)` in `packages/core` is the single source of truth for whether an action is `"automatic" | "batch" | "per_item" | "never"`. Actions like `broker_order` and `silent_source_calendar_write` are permanently `"never"` — connectors and UI should defer to this function rather than re-deriving policy.

**Every mutating route calls `isCrossSiteWrite` (`apps/web/lib/same-origin.ts`), first thing.** It refuses a POST/PUT/PATCH/DELETE whose `Origin` header names a different host than the one the browser actually addressed, and passes anything with no `Origin` at all — that is a local tool or the worker, not a browser form post, and this app is expected to support both. A red-team pass found seven routes that never called it (`commands`, `energy-observations`, `milestones`, `milestones/propose`, `codex/account`, `plans/[id]/approve`, `plans/changes/[id]`): a forged `Origin: https://evil.example` wrote real captures, energy observations and milestones, and against `codex/account` it started a real OAuth login. `apps/web/app/api/csrf-protection.test.ts` is the guard against this recurring — it is a **discovery** test, not a per-route one: `import.meta.glob` reads every `route.ts` under `app/api`, and every exported mutating method gets one forged-Origin request asserting 403. A route added without the check fails this test on day one instead of shipping unguarded, which is the property a per-route test cannot give you.

**Revoking auto-acceptance is keyed on the attempt, the same way a gate re-judgement is (see Gate 4 below).** `revokeAutoAcceptedWorkOrder` (`apps/web/lib/crossline.ts`) counts how many times this work order has already been revoked and builds `auto-acceptance-revoked:${workOrderId}:${attempt}` from that, rather than trusting the caller to number it. A work order that gets auto-accepted, revoked, re-judged and auto-accepted again is a normal cycle, and a fixed key reused the first revoke's on the second call: `appendEvent` silently no-ops on a repeated idempotency key, and the caller returned `{ ok: true }` without checking whether anything had actually changed — the person saw the page reload and believed the revoke worked, while the ledger never left `accepted`. `revokeAutoAcceptance` (`packages/db/src/index.ts`) now throws `REVOCATION_ALREADY_APPLIED` if `transitionWorkOrder` reports `changed: false`, mirroring `applyMilestoneGate`'s `GATE_ALREADY_APPLIED` — the store-level check is what turns a stale-key collision into an honest failure; the caller's attempt counter is what makes a genuine second revoke keep working instead of failing forever.

**The plan is one calendar** *(since 2026-10-10 only on Today; the plan page is long-range statistics — see 「Flywheel and views」)*. `apps/web/app/plan/page.tsx` + `plan-view.tsx` render day, week and month views (`apps/web/app/timeline/`) over one list of items: real calendar events, file-imported class meetings, and the blocks the scheduler placed for work orders. `timeline/model.ts` is pure (axis geometry, overlap packing into columns, the Monday-first month grid, conflict detection, the zone's current minute) and is where the tests are; `views.tsx` draws it — **day** is a horizontal 24-hour axis with one row per thing and a now line, **week** is seven columns sharing one vertical hour axis with a deadline strip on top, **month** is a block calendar of tags (colour is the line, outline says scheduled / calendar / file) with `+N` folding. `apps/web/lib/timeline.ts` builds the data: plan blocks, each live milestone's deadline from `milestone_anchors`, every confirmed `date_*` profile fact, and the person's unavailable hours as the complement of the availability windows. The same `CalendarPanel` is the day timeline on Today. **The plan takes effect by default** (docs/loop-and-scout.md §1, 2026-10-12): there are no plan versions to approve and no differences to settle. Migration 14 turned every `plan_changes` row into a `plan.change_archived` event and every older plan version into `plan.version_archived`, dropped the table and left each workspace one `plan_versions` row with status `current`; `importSnapshot` converts an older backup's `plan_changes` the same way instead of refusing it. `approvePlanVersion`, `resolvePlanChange`, the plan routes, the differences fold, the `calendar_stale` ask and the `approve_plan_changes` policy are gone. FullCalendar and the old B/C/A lenses are gone.

**One freeze model, locks per block** (`packages/core/src/schedule-frozen.ts`, `schedule.frozen`; 2026-10-12). A frozen block keeps its time while the rest is re-planned, for one of four reasons: `pinned` (the person started it — `lockOnStart` in `app/timeline/block-start.ts`, for scheduled and pencilled-in blocks alike), `locked` (the 「锁定在此时间」 button on the day panel, or the person dragging the block on their calendar app), `keep` (the local re-plan after an inserted event, a week) and `commit` (reserved for the commit window, §2). It replaced `schedule.pins` and `schedule.keep`, which `readFrozen` takes over once and deletes. The planner freezes the **segment**, not the task (`withoutFrozenMinutes` in `packages/planner/src/optimizer.ts`): a task keeps the rest of its minutes to place, work that must sit in one slot is never cut. It used to drop every task with any frozen block, so locking one segment of a long task left the rest unplanned — and the task's own validation then failed, sending the whole solve to the greedy pass. A frozen block keeps its own id (`scheduled:<order>[:<step>][:n]`); new segments skip ids a frozen block holds. The solver's answer is checked (`invalidBlocks`) on what **it** placed only: counting a frozen block as placed called a half-frozen step 「0 of 15 minutes」 and threw away every optimal plan on the real account for a day (2026-10-13; the week held 61 `keep` blocks); a rejected answer now logs `solver.answer_rejected` with the broken rule. Work that cannot start before the planned days end (`notBefore` past the last slot, or waiting on such work) is not a blocker — the window reaches it later. A `keep`/`commit` block a hard constraint now lands on moves; a `pinned`/`locked` one stays and is asked about (`locked_conflict`: next free time / tomorrow / keep the overlap → `overlapOk`). Re-planning everything (availability, packing numbers, weights, shares) clears `keep` only (`clearKept`). `POST /api/plan/pin` locks (`reason`, `overlapOk`), `DELETE` unlocks; both are undoable.

**What the person did on their calendar is judged once, for any provider** (`packages/connectors/src/calendar-edits.ts` `classifyCalendarEdits`). The publish ledger (`calendar.published`) now keeps title, start and end apart instead of one hash: the time moved → the worker locks that block at the new time (`apps/worker/src/calendar-publish.ts` writes `schedule.frozen`); only the title changed → not a lock, the time keeps following the plan under the person's title (`personTitle`); a future event deleted → `removedByPerson`, never created again; a finished task's leftover future event is deleted even if the person changed it (`forceRemove`). A ledger entry with only a `hash` is from before: the old rule holds until the event is found unchanged, when it is upgraded.

**The change summary** (`noteScheduleChanges` / `readChangeSummary` in `apps/web/lib/crossline.ts`, `schedule.changeSummary`, `app/schedule-summary.tsx`). When a `plan.reality_changed` has come in since the schedule was last read, each work block whose old time a newly added event covers becomes a line above Today's timeline — 「「作业 3」因 11:30 Sync up 后移 30 分钟」 — for a day. The re-plan is derived and has nothing to undo, so the button is 「钉回原位」: lock the block at its old time with the overlap accepted. Not written inside an impact preview.

**The completion check** (docs/loop-and-scout.md §3, 2026-10-13; `apps/web/lib/confirm.ts`, `app/confirm/[id]`, `/api/confirm/[id]`). Every work block of the day — deadline or not — is asked about once within thirty minutes of its end (`block_confirm` in `packages/core/src/nudges.ts`, which replaced `block_missed`); not one whose timer the person already stopped, one whose timer still runs, or at night (those go into the morning brief as 「昨晚 N 块待确认」, `unconfirmedLastNight` in `apps/worker/src/notify.ts`). The schedule keeps no past, so the two-minute nudge pass remembers the blocks it sees (`nudge.blocks:<day>`, `rememberBlocks`) and only one seen under way counts — the commit window holds a block at its time from fifteen minutes before, so every block that happened is seen, and one moved before it began is never asked about. The question goes to the desktop and the phone whatever page is open, and opens `/confirm/<message>`; nothing is written by following the link. The page: 按预估完成 / 超出预估 / 少于预估 / 未执行, then 15 / 30 / 60 minutes or a number, the screen record's estimate as one tap with the detail consent (`activityMinutes`), and evidence for a milestone-bound last step. A step that is not the last writes a closed session over the block's own times (`recordConfirmedSession`: ends an open one, else inserts one; `outcome: "confirmed"`, `reportedMinutes`) and marks the step done; the last step goes through `completeWorkOrderExecution` with the minutes, so `human.completed` writes the one duration observation (`one_tap`, or `activity`), and a task with no `family` is tagged `preparation` first so it is not left out. An active order with no open session opens one on start (`openSession`). The next day's `missed_block` skips anything answered (`block.confirmed`). Settings shows the estimate bias week by week (`weeklyBias`). `scripts/confirm-check.ts` runs the whole path on a copy of the real ledger with the person's ECE work (2026-10-13: 11 checks, the desktop notification shown once). **Since 2026-10-28 most blocks wait for the evening wrap-up** (next paragraph): on a day with available hours only a block whose task is due tomorrow or sooner is still asked about on its own (`NudgeEnding.dueDay`, `NudgeInput.wrap`) — **and since 2026-10-30 only when the passive signals infer it probably was not done** (`NudgeEnding.inferred`, `inferBlockOutcome`: work minutes in the block from its own timer, the screen's work categories and Codex, ≥60% done, <20% not done). Its push carries the message id (`PushMessage.confirm`) and the phone shows ✓做了 / ✗没做, which the service worker posts to `/api/confirm/<id>` with `via=push` without opening a page (`block.confirmed` gets `source: "push"`). The wrap-up prefills every row's inference (「推断」; 做了 only if the block could have finished its step, else 部分), 「推断都对」 writes them with `inferred: true`, and its one-tap 按预估完成 records a block worked under 70% of its minutes as 部分 (`bulkDoneRows` returns `outcome`). Its heading is 「今天排过的块」. **Work hands itself in** (`apps/web/lib/auto-close.ts`): once every step of the person's (not the assistant's, not a wait) is done and one was, the order is completed the 完成 way by the attention timer or right after a confirmed step, said once with 撤销 (`work_order.reopen` inverse: acceptance revoked, back to approved; never closed by itself twice).

**The weekly commitment, the evening wrap-up, 「全部按推荐」 and the weekly risk card** (the owner's decisions of 2026-10-28, after a day of 630 usable minutes against 1735 of work, four tasks 5–11 days overdue only ever rolled forward, and two risk counts on one screen; design and rules in `docs/rituals.md`, pure rules in `packages/core/src/rituals.ts`).
- **Weekly commitment** (`/commit`, `apps/web/lib/weekly-commit.ts`, `POST /api/commit`). The week's usable minutes come from `netCapacity` (the forecast's own capacity, now its own function); the week's work is open work on the person's board due by Sunday, started, or placed this week (`schedule.upcoming`), counted by `remainingMinutesOf`. `proposeWeeklyCommit` suggests 保 / 推 / 砍 (砍: never started, ≥7 days overdue, weight under 60; then started → due this week → heavier → sooner, kept while the minutes last). 「确认本周」 sets 推's `notBefore` to the next Monday (due day to that week's end when it fell before, `deferTarget`), archives 砍 with its reason, writes `schedule.weeklyCommit` and `week.committed`, all in one `withUndo`; 「预览影响」 runs `previewOverrides` (`lib/impact.ts`, several overrides at once). Asked (`weekly_commit`) from Sunday 17:00 to the end of Monday while the week is uncommitted and the suggestion pushes or cuts something; mid-week only once when a committed week's work grows more than 15% past its hours (`weeklyCommitDue`, `overloadAskedFor`). Today's day panel links 本周承诺 and 收工. The page (2026-10-28 redesign after `docs/plans/page-drafts-1028/commit.html`; `docs/plans/page-drafts-1028/impl-e.md`): usable, demand and kept hours over one bar that marks the usable line and hatches what runs past it; one line about last week (`lastWeekRecap`: that week's `week.committed` and the tasks first accepted in it), linking to the weekly review, which holds the detail; then 保 / 推到下周 / 不做了 as three columns, each row with why it was suggested there and icon buttons moving it to the other two.
- **Evening wrap-up** (`/wrap`, `apps/web/lib/wrap.ts`, `POST /api/wrap`). At the end of the day's last available window (`wrapTimeOf`) one `day_wrap` nudge (uncounted, never at night) opens the page: today's blocks seen under way, each 完成 / 部分 / 没做, written by `confirmBlockAt` (the completion check's writer, keyed `wrap:<day>:<block>`; `partial` records the block's time and half the step), then 「全部按推荐」 and 「收工」 (`wrap.done:<day>`). It replaced the per-block `block_confirm` pushes for every block not due by tomorrow; a day not wrapped up puts all its unconfirmed blocks in the next morning's brief (`unconfirmedLastNight`). The page (2026-10-28 redesign after `wrap.html`): four figures (timed today — timers overlapped with the day, pauses taken off in proportion —, blocks done, blocks to record, tomorrow's first block), each block with its time, the minutes timed inside it, line, task and step and a three-part control, 「全部按预估完成」 (the same call per block), 「全部按推荐」, and tomorrow's first three blocks (`loadTimeline`). `/wrap?day=YYYY-MM-DD` (`wrapDayOf`: the last seven days, else today) catches up on an earlier day the same way, keyed `wrap:<that day>:<block>`, `POST /api/wrap` with `day`; it has no 收工 and no tomorrow. **Per-source authority is the rule that keeps this safe.** Each `(account, calendar)` pair is a `CalendarSource` with its own delta cursor (`external_bindings.external_id` is the source key) and stamps `sourceKey` on every block it produces. Blocks imported before any of this carry no `sourceKey`, and may be dropped only when **every** selected source read its whole calendar that tick — `some(authoritative)` was wrong and did real damage: ticking a second calendar made that new source authoritative while the original stayed incremental, every legacy block was deleted as though the calendar had been emptied, and an approved plan forked into a draft full of 「日历中已不存在」 conflicts for events nobody had touched. **Planner (`packages/planner`):** Greedy `planSchedule` assigns WorkOrder-shaped inputs to availability slots while respecting deadline and continuity, returning typed capacity blockers. **Energy is how much an hour yields, not whether it may be used** (decided 2026-10-12, `packages/planner/src/optimizer.ts`): it was a hard filter, so work needing 「high」 had about three hours a day and a homework due the next day got nothing while those hours went to undated work. A slot one level short of the work's energy yields 0.8 of an hour (≈1.25× the time), two levels short 0.65 (≈1.5×) — assumptions, since the time-of-day literature shows real but moderate, task-dependent effects with no reliable percentage, to be calibrated against timed work; work is cut into even sessions of at most 90 minutes in a matching slot, 60 one level short and 45 two short, with ten-minute rests, several to a long slot; the solver counts the rests exactly (an integer `k` per slot where the work may run past one session). One level short is open to all work, two only to work with a deadline; each minute short costs a little in the objective, so a matching hour is always taken first but work is never left unplaced to avoid a weaker one. The greedy fallback (`fallbackSchedule`, tested directly) follows the same rules, matching slots first. Blocks placed short carry `efficiency` and the tag 「精力偏低」. **On the real account the solver runs out of its two seconds** (105 tasks, ~9,500 rows), so what the person sees is usually the greedy pass — three things follow (2026-10-12): a time-limited answer is compared with the greedy pass on the solver's own objective (`taskValue`/`worth`) and the better is kept (one had given an afternoon to line-share filler and left a homework due that night unplaced); the greedy pass keeps the gap only from the block just before in time (it used to take the last block placed anywhere, so one task on Friday emptied the whole week before it); and the relaxed run relaxes only the work with a deadline and what it waits on, then places everything else around it on the normal rules (relaxing the whole plan packed a day from 09:00 to 22:50 with no break), re-running the solver only if it had finished the first model in time. Its availability comes from `packages/core/src/availability.ts`: a weekly pattern (`AvailabilityWindow[]`) stored in the ledger under the `schedule.availability` setting — not an environment variable, because a backup that does not carry it restores a plan that cannot be rebuilt — which `availableSlots` turns into concrete slots by subtracting the calendar's own blocks and dropping anything under 15 minutes. `scheduledBlocks` in `apps/web/lib/crossline.ts` is its **first and only production caller**; until then the plan's AI lane was seeded example blocks computed from nothing, and those are gone. The schedule is **derived on read** from the work orders, so it cannot drift from them — **in two engines, with the optimal one cached by input** (docs/loop-and-scout.md §2, 2026-10-13): every read places with the greedy pass at once (`planSchedule(input, { solver: "none" })`, ~0.2 s on the real account) and asks for the optimal plan, which `apps/web/lib/optimal-schedule.ts` solves on a thread in the web process (`apps/web/solver-thread.mjs` + `lib/solver-pool.ts`: HiGHS outside the Next bundle, up to 20 s) and stores in `schedule.optimal` under `scheduleInputHash(input, epoch)` — the planner input without `now`, with the current slot's start and the self-reported energy as the epoch (`scheduleHashOf`). The next read of the same input uses it (blocks already over dropped); one solve per workspace at a time, only the latest input next. A preview (`withPreview`) neither reads nor asks for it, and `impact.ts` computes both sides inside a preview so the two engines' difference is never reported as a change's effect. The automatic publish waits up to a minute for the optimal plan (`blocksToPublish(…, { waitForOptimal })` → `pending`), then writes the greedy one; once solved it is published at once (`onOptimalWritten` → `publishNow`); the 发布 button never waits. **The commit window**: `commitSoon`, run by the two-minute nudge pass, freezes a block under way or starting within 15 minutes until it ends (`reason: "commit"`), so the optimal plan landing never moves what is about to start. The thread was verified in a production build (2026-10-13: `solver.optimal` in 1.1 s on the e2e board). `CROSSLINE_BACKGROUND_SOLVE=on|off` overrides the default (off under vitest and e2e); tests that need what only the solver does (line-share library work, the current slot ordered by the reported energy) read `settledPlan` (`lib/test-support.ts`). **Energy model v2, capacity and learned efficiency (2026-10-14; design and competitor notes in `docs/energy-and-ecosystem.md`).**
- **Model and its constants.** The model in `packages/core/src/energy-model.ts` now follows Åkerstedt, Folkard & Portin 2004 as published. The constants were read off the paper itself; an earlier recollection of W as `−5.72·e^(−1.51t)` was wrong.
  - S is carried across the last 14 days of sleep (`homeostat`). Below 12.5 it recovers at the rate it has at 12.5, which is what makes short nights add up.
  - A gap of more than 30 h between two records is filled with usual nights (`assumedNights`), never an invented all-nighter.
  - The model also has the 12-hour afternoon dip U and sleep inertia W = −5.7·e^(−0.65692t), via `alertnessAt`. `alertness(at, episode)` is gone.
  - `sleepNeed` is the upper quartile of recorded nights. `sleepDebt` uses RISE's weighting: last night 15%.
- **Day capacity.** `dayCapacity` comes from sleep debt, HRV and resting HR ≥1 SD off a ≥4-day/28-day baseline, and wrist temperature at least 0.5 °C up. It only ever lowers, to 0.7 at most, and its sizes are priors.
  - **Steps and daylight (2026-10-20).** The phone's `steps`/`daylightMin` in `health.daily` are candidate covariates (`phone.steps`, `phone.daylight` in `energyModel().covariates`, `use: "capacity"`): each report is paired with the figures filed under its local day and judged by `judgeCovariate`. Only an admitted one reaches `dayCapacity` (`admitted`, the fitted coefficient), and only a day ≥1 SD off the same ≥4-day/28-day baseline on the side that went with lower energy costs (≤0.1 each). They never move a reading of now. Settings → 精力 shows the latest day's figures and each verdict.
  - It reaches the planner as `PlanningInput.capacityByDay`. That holds only today's work **without a deadline**, in both the greedy pass and the solver's `capacity_<day>` rows; the emergency run ignores it.
  - It cannot be folded into `maxDailyShare`, which also picks the mode and is forced to 1 in an emergency.
- **Learned efficiency.** Scheduled blocks carry `energy {slot, need, efficiency}`. `rememberBlocks` and `confirmBlock` copy it, with `blockMinutes`, into `block.confirmed`.
  - `packages/core/src/efficiency.ts` fits the factors for one and two levels short relative to matching slots, shrunk with K=5 towards 0.8/0.65.
  - `personalEfficiency` passes `PlanningInput.efficiency` only when the rolling backtest beats the assumed factors by `PROMOTE_MARGIN`.
  - **Blocks that produced nothing (2026-10-20).** A block answered 「未执行」 and one seen under way on a past day (`nudge.blocks:<day>`, 28 days) with no timer that day and no answer go in as `done: false`. `fitEfficiencyWithMisses` counts them as zero yield (linear means, same shrinkage and bounds); `learnedEfficiency` uses those factors only when `backtestMisses` (squared yield error over every short block, misses included) beats both the assumed and the done-only factors by `PROMOTE_MARGIN`. An unanswered block may have been done silently, which is why it is gated, not assumed.

**Skill wire contracts** all live in `packages/core`: `analysis.ts`, `flywheel-schema.ts` (findings + candidate relations), `materials-schema.ts`, `milestone-gate-schema.ts` (gate result, criteria proposal, milestone proposal), and `reality.ts` (the pure calendar-vs-plan diff). Keeping them there is what stops a Skill's shape and its trust checks drifting apart.

A contract item is `{ kind, description, why, required }` in both `materialContract` and `evidenceContract`. `description` is the checklist line (≤300, no `{}[]`, refused if it lands exactly on the limit — that means it was cut off); `why` is the reason and carries no punctuation rule, because a reason is a sentence and sentences quote things. `why` exists because every split in the schema has a `rationale` and a contract item had none: the model had something real to say and one field to say it in, so it packed the reason into the name and filled whatever cap was set — 4000, then 300, then 200, then 80, each treated as a target. With `why` in place the names came back at 6–8 characters. The limit then went **back up** to 300, against instinct: constrained decoding concentrates repetition at structured-field terminals, so a tighter schema makes terminal degeneration *more* likely rather than less, and the tightening had been pushing the wrong way. The primary defence is now detect-and-retry in `runCodexSkill` — when `validateOutput` rejects an answer the rejection message is sent back as a **second turn on the same thread** (which already carries the context, so it is cheaper than a fresh run), at most twice, and the count lands in `audit.repairs` and `StoredCapture.audit`. A repair rate that stays above zero means the schema or the prompt needs work, not more retries. Two normalizations run before validation, both of which only ever remove text that repeats text already present: a tail of one character repeated eight or more times is trimmed, and a sentence whose whole second half already appeared earlier is dropped — both are now last-resort cleanup behind the retry, not the primary defence they used to be. `required` on `materialContract` is what lets the preparer tell "I cannot start without this" from "this would help" — without it one nice-to-have the person had not supplied blocked the entire preparation and they got nothing. `validateAnalysisProposal` names the offending quote or contract line in its blocker, because a bare code gave the person nothing to retry against.

**One structured-output loop, not two** (`packages/codex/src/skill-runner.ts`). A fresh run and a continued conversation used to carry their own near-identical copies of the notification handler, the turn driver and the repair loop — the file said so, and said why it did not merge them. They now share `runTurnsUntilValid` (prompt → final message → parse → the caller's validator → on any rejection, the rejection goes back on the same thread, at most twice), `openScope` (timeout + cancellation) and `failureResult` (the blocker vocabulary); `startIsolatedThread` holds everything that makes an unattended run safe. **A final message that is not JSON now counts as a rejection and is repaired** — before, `JSON.parse` failing ended the run at once with `SKILL_SCHEMA_INVALID`, so the cut-off and repeating-itself outputs this loop exists for were exactly the ones it never saw. `packages/core/src/structured-output.ts` (`structuredOutputProblems`) states the strict-mode rules (every property required, `additionalProperties:false`, `anyOf` for unions, depth and size limits) and `structured-output.test.ts` checks every exported `*_SCHEMA` against them by discovery, so a new Skill's schema is held to them the day it exists. `scripts/structured-output-check.ts` is the real-run measurement; `docs/structured-output.md` is the guide.

**Two clean-ups that exist because of one real run** (`scripts/structured-output-check.ts`, 2026-10-03; numbers and the full account in `docs/structured-output.md`). (1) A model that ends a free-text field with alternating zero-width characters, a stray Malayalam letter and a leaked token got through the old "same character eight times" trim and was **stored**; `cleanAnswerStrings` (`packages/core/src/text-hygiene.ts`, applied to every Skill's parsed answer, skipping `*quote` keys because those are compared with the person's own text) strips invisible characters and `trimLeakedTail` cuts a foreign-script/bracket tail off the analysis contract strings. (2) A Skill that stops and says why (`*_BLOCKED`) is **not** repaired: the repair loop was sending 「缺少待整理材料」 back as an error, and the model answered by deleting its blocker and inventing the material. Every degenerate shape found leaves a fixture in `apps/worker/src/testing/fixtures/`.

**Milestones are per project, and lines are linked** (found by walking the real account, whose 主线 page showed milestones for one of four research projects and no cross-line link at all). `validateMilestonePlans` takes `requiredProjects` — projects with three or more confirmed facts — and refuses an answer that leaves one out (`MILESTONE_PROJECT_MISSING`); chains, stage order and `dependsOn` are checked per `(line, project)`, one project's chain is at most five, a line at most twelve, and a `projectId` that is not one of the line's projects is refused with the valid ones. **The architect is asked one line at a time** (`createMilestoneProposer`: `lineContext` gives each run only its line's identity, projects, facts, templates and diligence, so the identity it may name and the chains it owes are about that line alone). What succeeds is saved and what does not is reported — `skipped` plus a line in `assumptions` naming the line and why — instead of one line's trouble throwing the others away; a line the profile says too little about (fewer than two facts) is named as not scheduled; old untouched drafts are archived only in lines that got new ones. A blocker next to real milestones is kept as an assumption, and only an answer with no milestone is a stop. *(Superseded 2026-10-10: relations now come from the artifact plan, see 「Flywheel and views」.)* The links between lines were then a second, small run in a different mode (`crossline.milestone-relations.v1`, `MILESTONE_RELATIONS_SCHEMA`/`validateRelationsProposal`): milestones by id, **different lines only**, with `explanation`, `refutedWhen`, `sourceFactIds`; each is stored as a `candidate` relation whose evidence is the profile facts it rests on, so the 主线 page draws it dotted and the person approves or rejects it. A failed links run never takes the milestones with it. A milestone must be an **event, not a state** (`MILESTONE_NOT_CONCRETE`: 「课程学习保持可完成状态」, 「稳定推进」, 「做好准备」 are refused with what an event looks like — an exam with a score, a submission, a person's reply — and a target saying 保持 needs a number). The interview's `flywheel_*` facts were only ever profile facts — no milestone existed yet to connect them. `scripts/user-journey-check.ts` replays the whole path with the real Codex on a throwaway database (files → interview → dates → milestones → approval → the blocked capture → analysis); its checks are the ones that were false on the real account.

**Captures are analysed without a click, and without a milestone.** The worker tick runs `capturesAwaitingAutoAnalysis` (`apps/worker/src/analysis.ts`): captures never attempted, while the quota allows core work (it runs in economy too), at most three per workspace per tick. Every line is offered and approved milestones are offered for binding when they exist; with none, the work is filed under its line with an empty `milestoneId` (the output schema and the Skill always allowed this — a GTD next action with no project). Requiring an approved milestone first made a new account's first day a dead end and was removed on 2026-10-10; a capture an older version blocked with `ANALYSIS_APPROVED_MILESTONE_REQUIRED` is re-picked by the tick. An unbound task shows 「未挂里程碑」. The context takes up to `APPROVED_MILESTONES_LIMIT` (60) approved milestones, nearest deadline first: it took the list helper's default of 32, and from the day the owner's account passed 32 approved milestones every capture analysis was refused as `ANALYSIS_CONTEXT_INVALID` (found 2026-10-21 when two to-dos from a recording stayed blocked; 收集分析 showed one run in a week). A capture blocked by that or by the old milestone requirement is re-picked by the tick (`REPICKED_BLOCKERS`); one blocked for any other reason waits for the person. The analysis validator also refuses an `identityId` that is not one of the lines (a real task had been filed under 「\」).

**Every quantity is worked backwards from an outcome and carries a margin** (`packages/core/src/milestone-plan.ts`, `analysis.ts`, `packages/planner/src/forecast.ts`). A milestone's `plan` has `outcome` (the result the person wants) and `derivation` (the back-calculation in numbers); a `threshold` measure has `bareLine` (the value that only just reaches the outcome) next to `value` (the line actually set). `thresholdProblem` refuses a `value` that is not on the safe side of `bareLine` by at least `MARGIN_RATIO` (3%; a score may sit at 100) — `MILESTONE_NO_MARGIN` — and `validateMilestonePlans` refuses a threshold with no derivation containing a number (`MILESTONE_DERIVATION_MISSING`) or a milestone with no stated outcome (`MILESTONE_OUTCOME_UNSTATED`). The derivation is appended to the stored rationale and `bareLine` is shown beside the line on the milestone page; `bareLine` is optional in the stored type because hand-written and older criteria have none. Estimates: the analysis works back from the `evidenceContract` to its steps, `p50Minutes` is their unpadded sum, and the margin lives only in `p85Minutes` (`ANALYSIS_ESTIMATE_MARGIN_INVALID` unless it is 1.2–3x p50). The scheduler already reserves p85 for low-confidence work; `forecastGoals` now reports `slackDays` and flags a goal whose p85 finish is less than `DEADLINE_MARGIN_DAYS` (2) before its deadline.

**Targets are proposed by the model from the person's long-term goals, and changed by them in one click** (`milestone-plan.ts`, `milestone-proposer.ts`, `apps/web/lib/targets.ts`, `apps/web/app/milestones/milestone-drawer.tsx` `CourseDrawer`). Two earlier versions got this wrong in opposite directions: the architect assumed a course's total grade and derived every score line from a number nobody had chosen, and the next version made the person answer a question list before anything existed to react to. Now the architect, which has the person's goals, priorities and grade weights, proposes the target itself: `targetProposals: [{projectId, value, why}]` in its answer, stored by `persistTargetProposals` as an **inferred** `target_grade` fact with the reason in its value (`值（暂定：理由）`) and never over an existing one. `validateMilestonePlans` refuses a graded milestone in a course (`isCourse`: a `grading` fact, or quizzes/homework dated in its own files — a real interview recorded a syllabus's dates and never its percentages) when no target is on record and none is proposed in the same answer (`MILESTONE_TARGET_UNPROPOSED`), and when its `sourceFactIds` do not cite `<line>/<project>/target_grade` (`MILESTONE_TARGET_NOT_CITED`), so every score line traces to the one number the person can edit. On the Milestones timeline a course's target is the ★ at the end of its chain; its drawer shows the target with its reason, 批准 (confirms it), grade options or free text, and 「修改并重算草稿」 — `POST /api/targets` confirms the new value, then `POST /api/milestones/propose` with `only:[line]` redoes that line's untouched drafts (approved milestones are never touched; their score lines are edited in place in each milestone's drawer, with `bareLine` beside the input and a warning when an edit leaves under 3% margin). Proposed targets still waiting count in 待确认 (`pending.targets`). What is still asked as a question, because it is a fact the model cannot propose (`profileGaps`): a line that rests only on the interviewer's guesses (`LINE_UNCONFIRMED`, blocks 「ready」, `withGapQuestions` turns a premature 「ready」 back into 「ask」 and adds the question with options) and a graded part a file marks TBD (`DATE_UNDATED`, asked once, blocks nothing). Answers to those code-built questions are read straight out of the `【question】选择：label` message (`answeredGapFacts`) and written as confirmed facts before the model runs.

**TBD is not a date, and not nothing** (`date-schema.ts` `undated`, `latestFileDates` in `packages/db`). The ECE 555 schedule's last row is `TBD · Final Project Report · Due`; the date extractor rightly refused to invent a day, and the 25% project then had no milestone at all because the chain was built from dated items. The interview turn now returns such rows in `fileDates.undated` (each citing its sentence, checked like a date), they are stored in the `dates.extracted` payload, counted as a graded part by `MILESTONE_COURSE_PARTS_MISSING`, and the architect gives them a milestone with a provisional deadline marked 「日期待定」. A date that is genuinely absent from the files is the person's to supply (the question above), not ours to find.

**The assistant is a second way of using Codex, with a shell** (design and decisions in `docs/assistant-agent.md`; `packages/codex/src/agent-mode.ts`, `skill-runner.ts` `startAgentThread`, `apps/worker/src/agent-task.ts`, `packages/core/src/agent-guard.ts`, `agent-summary.ts`, Skill `crossline-agent`). Structured Skills stay read-only with no tools; the assistant runs the same runner with `agent` set: `sandbox: danger-full-access`, `approvalPolicy: untrusted`, the task's own directory as `cwd`, every runtime tool on. `untrusted` is what makes the runtime ask before each command that is not on its own short list of harmless ones, and `JsonLineRpcClient.onServerRequest` (new — the client used to ignore requests from the runtime, so an approval would have hung to the timeout, and an id clash could resolve one of our own pending calls) hands each request to `registerAgentThread`, which routes by `threadId` to the run that owns it; `createAgentAnswerer` asks the guard. **The guard is a tripwire, not a sandbox**: it refuses secret paths, sending/publishing/paying/system changes, deleting a root, and writes outside the task directory, and asks about installs, pushes, uploads and recursive deletes; a script written to get round a text check will get round it, so the real protection is the rest (task directory or git worktree on `ai/<taskId>`, hashes of the inputs before and after, every command and file change recorded, an artifact check that rejects a claimed file that does not exist, and the last step of anything that leaves the machine being the person's). Verified on the real runtime on 2026-10-05: a learning-material task ran in 222s with 5 commands and left the originals untouched, and a task told to copy a `.env` into a note had its `Get-Content` of it refused by `secret.path` while the chart half of the task finished. Kinds of work (`AGENT_CATEGORIES`) are approved once (`agent.categories` setting) and earn trust only through accepted, fully checked results (`CLEAN_RUNS_TO_TRUST`); a rejected or failed one sends the kind back to review-all. The ledger keeps `agent.task.*` provenance (commands, changes, artifact hashes, verification), never content; the assistant has its own lane so a long task does not hold the capture analysis or the gate. **Phase 2 wired it into the board** (`packages/db/src/agent.ts`, `packages/core/src/agent-dispatch.ts`, `apps/worker/src/agent-{dispatch,service}.ts`, `apps/web/lib/agent.ts`, `/api/agent/*`, 待确认 `#assistant` and 设置 → 自动化与助手; the 「交给 Agent」 button on a task card). **Skills have no shell, and that is evidence, not neglect.** `scripts/shell-sandbox-probe.ts` ran read, write and network commands under four permission setups: every command was refused before execution, because on Windows without the Windows sandbox backend a restricted-filesystem profile under `approvalPolicy: never` forbids all commands (`vendor/codex/codex-rs/core/src/exec_policy.rs`). Enabling the backend is a system-level ACL setup, and even enabled Windows can enforce only a deny-read list, not "this one folder". Do not add an `allowShell` option to a structured Skill without redoing that probe; the assistant below is the deliberate exception, run on `danger-full-access` rather than through a restricted sandbox.

**The Phase 4 acceptance bullets are a test** (`e2e/design-audit.spec.ts`): every page at desktop and phone width must be Arial throughout, have no text under 12px, no text under 7:1 contrast, and no sideways scroll. It found real defects when first run — the line colours were 5.4–6.7:1 as text, the day-timeline blocks had black text on purple in light mode (a dark-mode rule missing its media query), and Today and Plan were 598 and 570px too wide on a phone because the calendar header's grid track sized to its content. `AUDIT_VERBOSE=1` prints what each page violates and which element pokes out.

**Skill deadlines are one hour per turn, not per run — finishing the task comes before speed, and the limit is there only to catch a run that has truly hung.** `openScope` (`packages/codex/src/skill-runner.ts`) gives every RPC and every turn its own `timeoutMs` window (`60 * 60_000` in every runner); it used to be one deadline from first call to last, so a long first answer left the repair that the validator then asked for a few seconds, and a run that was nearly right timed out with nothing to show (the interview did this to a real run). What bounds a run is the repair count (two). Milestone design is also asked one line at a time (see the milestone paragraph): in one answer it wrote ~11k tokens at ~4.5 minutes a turn. The web routes that wait on a run allow three hours (six for milestone design, which is several runs); the worker finishes and writes its outcome even if the page gave up. **Slow is allowed, silent is not.** A stalled turn is interrupted and resent; the interrupted turn's own late `turn/completed` is ignored (its id goes into `abandoned`), because it used to end the resent turn as 「已取消」 — two real assistant runs were lost that way after 96 minutes and 2.5 hours. The limit above is a ceiling, not the thing that catches a hang: `runTurnsUntilValid` also watches for *any* event from the runtime about the thread (`lastEventAt`), and a turn with none for 20 minutes (`CROSSLINE_SKILL_IDLE_MINUTES`) is interrupted and the same prompt sent again, twice at most (`audit.stalls`; then `SKILL_STALLED`). It exists because a real interview run sat for over an hour with the runtime logging nothing at all, and a startup-line milestone run took 43 minutes for 7k tokens — a stalled turn waits out the whole hour otherwise, and a long productive turn is never cut because it keeps producing events. **Measure, then tune:** Settings → 模型用量 shows each Skill's average **and longest** run, `scripts/user-journey-check.ts` prints the wall-clock of every step, so a limit is set from observed times and not guessed. When a limit and a finished answer disagree, lengthen the limit.

**One quota gate for every background pass** (`backgroundSpend`/`spendAllows` in `packages/core/src/cost.ts`, `apps/worker/src/spend.ts`). There used to be eight copies of the check in the worker, plus diligence, manualbook, the assistant and the routing provider, and they disagreed: an unreadable quota stopped the passes, counted as normal for routing, as spent for materials, and was ignored by the assistant; economy mode stopped the capture analysis even though that Skill never steps down. An unreadable quota (an API-key login reports no windows) pauses background work and writes `BACKGROUND_BLOCKER`, which the web asks about as `background_paused` — never a silent stop. The ask and Settings → 后台任务 say what still works and what waits (`degradedService`: the schedule, calendar, reminders, timing, completion and manual edits need no model; analysis, step plans, tutorials, research and the assistant do; a fully spent quota (`RATE_LIMIT_REACHED`, or `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`, which the policy writes only at 100%) also stops the input box and the interview) and when it comes back (`BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` now carries the fullest window's `resetsAt` too). Important work the person does themselves is planned with the web open (`research: true` in the input, `allowWebSearch`): the Skill researches the task's background and the best known way to do it first, plans on that, and asks only what the person alone can answer. `settleOldQuestions` (on the task pass) applies the same rule to stored questions.
